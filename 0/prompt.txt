[18] assistant: You can prevent StubHub from using future code, but that code cannot honestly be called open source. Open-source licenses must allow use by every person and company without discrimination. [OSI states this explicitly](https://opensource.org/osd).

For StubHub, I’d use this approach:

1. **Keep unreleased code private until licensing is settled.**
2. Have an open-source/IP attorney draft a **source-available license with a Restricted Parties schedule**.
3. Define the restricted party broadly enough to include:

   > StubHub Holdings, Inc.; StubHub, Inc.; viagogo entities; their parents, subsidiaries, affiliates, successors, and assigns; and persons acting on their behalf or primarily for their benefit.

   StubHub Holdings’ current subsidiaries include StubHub, Inc., viagogo entities, Ticket Utils, and others, according to its [2026 SEC subsidiary filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1337634/000162828026014844/a211listingofsubsidiaries.htm). 4. Prohibit direct and indirect reproduction, modification, deployment, distribution, and contractor use by restricted parties.
5. Require copyright assignment—or an appropriately drafted contributor agreement—before accepting contributions, so other copyright holders cannot undermine future licensing decisions.
6. Register important releases with the U.S. Copyright Office. Timely registration materially improves U.S. enforcement options. [The Copyright Office explains the benefits here](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/).

Do not simply append “except StubHub” to MIT, Apache, or AGPL and continue describing the result as that standard license. It becomes a custom, non-OSI license and can create dependency-compatibility problems.

For Callisto specifically, the existing versions are already available to StubHub:

- `callisto-model`, `callisto-format`, and `callisto-vcs` use MIT or Apache-2.0.
- The other crates use AGPL-3.0-only.

Apache grants an expressly irrevocable copyright license, and GPL/AGPL rights are irrevocable while their conditions are met. Those existing versions cannot simply be recalled. StubHub could continue using or forking them. You could restrict only future code that you have authority to relicense, and existing contributors may need to consent.

If you want to remain genuinely open source, your strongest practical alternative is AGPL-3.0-only. It can require source availability when a modified program is offered through a network, but it does **not** prevent StubHub from using the software—especially unmodified or purely internally.

So the hard choice is:

- **Open source:** StubHub may use it under the same rules as everyone else.
- **Prevent StubHub use:** adopt a lawyer-drafted source-available license for future versions and accept the reduced adoption and ecosystem compatibility.

Because this involves a named former employer and potentially your employment invention-assignment agreement, have counsel review that agreement before changing licenses or asserting ownership. Work created within the scope of employment may belong to the employer under U.S. work-for-hire rules.
